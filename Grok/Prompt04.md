**Entry-level jobs in robotics and cybernetics** exist in a growing field, though pure "cybernetics" roles are uncommon as standalone titles. Cybernetics principles (control systems, feedback, communication) are typically embedded in robotics, automation, control engineering, and systems roles.

### Common Entry-Level Robotics Jobs
Here are typical starting positions (US-focused data as of 2026):

| Job Title                          | Typical Requirements                          | Responsibilities                              | Approx. Salary Range (US) |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Robotics Technician / Automation Technician** | Associate degree, certificate, or HS diploma + training; hands-on skills | Install, maintain, troubleshoot, and operate robots | $50K–$80K (often hourly $22–$43/hr) |
| **Robot Operator**                | HS diploma/GED; on-the-job training           | Set up, monitor, and basic maintenance of robotic systems | $40K–$60K+ |
| **Junior / Entry-Level Robotics Engineer** | Bachelor's in Robotics, ME, EE, CS, or related | Assist in design, testing, programming (ROS, Python/C++), integration | $85K–$115K (median ~$105K) |
| **Junior Automation Engineer**    | Bachelor's + internships/projects             | Support PLCs, controls, system integration    | $70K–$100K+ |
| **Controls Engineer (Entry-Level)** | Bachelor's in Engineering (controls focus)    | Design, program, and troubleshoot control systems (foundational to cybernetics) | $75K–$110K |
| **Field Service / Support Technician** | Technical certification or degree             | On-site robot repair, customer support        | $55K–$85K + benefits |
| **Test / Quality Technician**     | Relevant degree or experience                 | Test prototypes, data collection, validation  | $60K–$90K |

**Note**: True entry-level engineering roles often prefer candidates with internships, capstone projects, or personal robotics projects (e.g., Arduino/Raspberry Pi, ROS experience).

### Cybernetics-Focused Entry Points
- **Control Systems Engineer (Entry-Level)**: Heavily overlaps with cybernetics (feedback loops, stability, automation). Many openings in manufacturing, aerospace, and process industries.
- Research assistant or junior roles in labs working on adaptive systems, bio-inspired control, or human-machine interfaces.
- Roles in prosthetics, autonomous systems, or complex system modeling.

Pure cybernetics jobs are rarer and more academic/research-oriented; most enter via robotics, mechatronics, or electrical/control engineering.

### How to Get Started
1. **Education**:
   - **Technician roles**: Certificates, associate degrees, or apprenticeships (e.g., in mechatronics or robotics automation).
   - **Engineering roles**: Bachelor's degree (Robotics, Mechanical/Electrical/Computer Engineering, Mechatronics). Master's helps for stronger competition.
2. **Key Skills**:
   - Programming: Python, C++, ROS/ROS 2.
   - Controls: PID, feedback systems, PLCs.
   - Hardware: Sensors, actuators, basic electronics.
   - Tools: MATLAB/Simulink, CAD, simulation software.
   - Soft skills: Problem-solving, documentation, teamwork.
3. **Experience Builders**:
   - Internships (highly recommended; robotics interns often earn $25–$55/hour).
   - University robotics clubs, competitions (e.g., FIRST, RoboCup), personal projects.
   - Certifications (e.g., FANUC, Universal Robots, or control systems).

### Job Market Notes
- **Demand** is solid, especially in manufacturing, logistics, automotive, aerospace, and defense. Hundreds to thousands of entry-level postings appear on LinkedIn/Indeed.
- **Locations**: Strongest in manufacturing hubs (Midwest, Southeast), tech centers (Boston, SF Bay Area, Pittsburgh, Austin), and automation-heavy regions.
- **Pathways**: Technician → Specialist/Engineer; or Engineer via internships → full-time junior role. Many companies offer structured training for new grads.

The field rewards **hands-on experience** alongside theory. Start with technician or internship roles if you lack a degree, or target new-grad engineering programs. Check LinkedIn, Indeed, BuiltIn, company career pages (e.g., Boston Dynamics, Tesla, Siemens, ABB, Universal Robots), and university job boards for current openings.
