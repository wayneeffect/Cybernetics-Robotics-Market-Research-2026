**Cybernetics** is the interdisciplinary study of **control, communication, and feedback** in living organisms, machines, and organizations. It explores how systems regulate themselves, process information, and adapt to achieve goals.

### Core Definition
The term was coined in 1948 by mathematician **Norbert Wiener** in his book *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine*. It comes from the Greek word *kybernetes*, meaning "steersman" or "governor" (the same root as "government").

Cybernetics focuses on **circular causal systems** — systems where actions influence the environment, and the resulting changes (feedback) loop back to influence future actions. This applies equally to:
- Biological systems (e.g., how your body maintains temperature or balance)
- Mechanical/electronic systems (e.g., thermostats, autopilots)
- Social and economic systems (e.g., markets, organizations)

### Key Concepts
- **Feedback Loops**:
  - *Negative feedback*: Stabilizing (e.g., a thermostat turning off when the room is warm enough; homeostasis in the body).
  - *Positive feedback*: Amplifying (e.g., a microphone squealing or population booms/crashes).
- **Information and Communication**: How signals are transmitted, encoded, and used for decision-making.
- **Control**: Mechanisms that guide a system toward a goal despite disturbances.
- **Black Box**: Analyzing systems based on inputs/outputs without needing full internal knowledge.
- **Homeostasis**: Self-regulation to maintain stability.
- **Adaptation and Learning**: How systems improve over time through feedback.

### Major Influences and History
- **Origins (1940s)**: Emerged from the Macy Conferences involving Wiener, John von Neumann, Warren McCulloch, Walter Pitts, Gregory Bateson, and others. It bridged mathematics, neuroscience, engineering, and social sciences.
- **1940s–1960s**: Golden age — influenced early AI, computer science, robotics, and systems theory.
- **Later developments**:
  - **Second-order cybernetics** (Heinz von Foerster, Humberto Maturana): Emphasizes the observer as part of the system (self-referential).
  - **Cybernetic systems** in management, ecology, and architecture.
  - Strong influence on cognitive science, biology (autopoiesis), and modern fields like complexity science.

### Relationship to Robotics and Other Fields
Cybernetics is closely related to **robotics** — many foundational ideas in robot control (sensors → processing → actuators with feedback) come directly from cybernetics. However:
- **Cybernetics** is more abstract and general: It studies *any* regulatory system.
- **Robotics** applies these principles (plus mechanics, AI, etc.) to build physical machines.
- Overlaps with: **Systems theory**, **control theory**, **information theory**, **AI**, **biomimetics**, and **cognitive science**.

Modern descendants include:
- **Systems engineering**
- **Control engineering** (PID controllers in everything from cars to drones)
- **Biofeedback** and neuroscience
- **Organizational cybernetics** (e.g., Stafford Beer’s Viable System Model)
- Aspects of **machine learning** and **reinforcement learning** (learning from rewards/feedback)

### Real-World Examples
- Thermostats and cruise control in cars
- Biological reflexes and immune responses
- Economic supply/demand adjustments
- Self-balancing robots (e.g., Segway) or drones with stabilization
- Neural networks (inspired by early cybernetic models of neurons)

In essence, **cybernetics is the science of how things steer themselves** — whether a single cell, a robot, a company, or an entire ecosystem. It provides a unifying language for understanding purposeful behavior across domains. While less hyped today as a standalone field, its principles are deeply embedded in almost every modern technology and scientific discipline.
